## 1. Research design

* **Paradigm.** A quantitative comparative experiment with qualitative error and cluster inspection, as in the proposal (`Project/481013386_MohammedKhalifah_5102_A1-X.docx` §3.1).
* **Unit of analysis.** One public Arabic app-review text.
* **Task as implemented.** Two decisions are made for each text:
  1. **Gate:** is it a *technical complaint* (complaint / not complaint)?
  2. **Category:** if it is, which primary operational failure category applies? The categories are AUTH, AVAIL, PERF, TRANS, UX and OTHER (`configs/taxonomy.yaml`).
* **Deviation from the proposal.** The proposal (§3.3) restricted the classifier to complaint records only. Because the deployed system must also reject non-complaints, the implementation added the gate. It reports gate, category-only and end-to-end (7-way: NO + six categories) metrics. [F]
* **Primary-category rule** (`reports/annotation_guide.md`, unchanged from the proposal): assign the first clearly stated blocking failure; otherwise the most emphasised issue; otherwise OTHER. A multi-issue flag is stored, but multi-label classification is out of scope. [F]
* **Symptom, not cause.** Categories classify the stated symptom, not a verified engineering cause.

**Blinding and storage.**
* **What coders saw.** Only an opaque id and the privacy-masked text. They never saw the sentiment, the AURA label or the app name.
* **Adjudication.** The adjudicator saw both labels plus the guide, for disagreements only. In A4 these were anonymised and shuffled.
* **Storage.** `label_a`, `label_b`, `label_final` and `adjudicated` are stored separately (`data/annotation/labels.parquet`, `data/a4/labels_a4.parquet`).
* **Uncertainty flag.** An item is uncertain if any coder or the adjudicator set `uncertain`. Rates were 0.3397 (A4 train) and 0.3419 (A4 holdout).

### 3.2 Guide versions and pilots

* **v1.0-draft → v1.0.**
  * A 100-item pilot gave complaint κ 0.740, joint κ 0.669 and category κ 0.692, with 24 disagreements.
  * Haiku over-used `unclear` for insults and gibberish. Rules 8–11 and `unclear`/`no` clarifications were added, and the guide was frozen at v1.0. [R]
* **v1.1** (frozen in commit `7341ed7`, before any A4 labelling) added clarifications only: code-switching and rules 12–14. Category definitions were unchanged. [F]
* **Coder-B substitution (A4.5 pilot).** Fable agreed with the v1 final labels at κ 0.80, against 0.70 for Haiku, so Fable became coder B (`data/a4/coder_check/fable_pilot.jsonl`). [F]

### 3.3 Quality incidents and corrections (disclosed)

1. **v1 pilot.** Coder B's first run produced no labels: it wrote an unrelated plan. It was re-run with a strict labelling-only prompt and returned 100/100 valid. [R]
2. **v1 batch `main_05`.** Coder B's output was degenerate: 30 complaints, against 92–124 in the other batches.
   * It was quarantined in `data/annotation/out/rejected/`, re-coded (110 complaints) and re-adjudicated. [R]
3. **A4.** The Fable coders were auto-restarted after a usage-limit interruption. The final files come from the completed rerun, and adjudication ran afterwards. [R]
4. **cleanlab confident learning** (A4.2): 5-fold out-of-fold char TF-IDF + calibrated LR (7-way) over v1 train+val (n = 1,665) flagged **239** items.
   * The flag rate was 0.3347 among adjudicated items, against 0.1748 overall (`results/a4/label_issues_summary.json`). [F]
   * Inspection showed that most flags reflect model weakness (out-of-fold macro-F1 0.465) rather than label errors. No confidence-only relabelling was done. [R]
5. **Re-review** of the 239: 200 were blind-confirmed and 39 adjudicated. **32 labels changed**, mostly AVAIL→OTHER (11) and UX→OTHER (6) (`agreement_a4.json: v1_rereview`). The **v1 test labels were never modified** (`tests/test_a4.py::test_v1_test_labels_untouched`). [F]

### 3.4 Agreement

The full table is in `ALL_EXPERIMENTAL_RESULTS.md` §14.
* v1 overall: complaint κ 0.781, joint κ 0.713, category κ 0.740; 383 adjudicated.
* A4 holdout: complaint κ 0.873, joint κ 0.843, category κ 0.880; 124 adjudicated. [F]

### 3.5 What is human-labelled?

* **Human labels exist only in AURA-Classification** (the authors' `bug_report` / `improvement_request` / `rating` / `others` labels). They are used as an external **proxy** for the gate: v1 κ 0.722 between the AI labels and `bug_report` on 293 overlapping items. They form a different construct.
* **Every reference label in this project is AI-assisted.** That covers all labels used for training, validation, test, holdout, synthetic verification and system mentions.
* **No human spot-check was conducted.** The 100-item check recommended in `reports/integration_notes.md` remains open.
* **These labels must never be described as human gold.**
* **Proposal deviation.** This departs from the proposal's plan of two Arabic-proficient human coders, 20% double-coding and human discussion of disagreements (§3.5). It was a user decision, recorded in `reports/integration_notes.md`.

## 4. Text processing

| Variant | Operations | Source |
|---|---|---|
| P0 (minimal + privacy) | remove control and zero-width characters; normalise whitespace; mask URLs, e-mails, @mentions; mask Saudi mobiles (05x / +966 / 00966, incl. Arabic-Indic digits) and any run of ≥ 7 digits | `src/text.py` [F] |
| P1 (P0 + orthography) | alif variants → ا; ya/alif-maqsura and ta-marbuta handling; remove tatweel and diacritics; reduce repeated letters; Arabic-Indic → ASCII digits | `src/text.py` [F] |
| P2 (stop words / light stemming) | **not implemented** | [P] |

* **Negation is preserved.** Negation particles (لا، ما، مش، مو، ليس، مافي) are never removed, and there is no stop-word removal.
* **Input to MARBERT.** P0 text; the tokenizer handles the rest.
* **Duplicate detection** (`src/dedup.py`): an exact hash on P1 text, plus near-duplicates by char 3–5-gram TF-IDF cosine ≥ 0.85, merged by union-find across both corpora.
  * 29.9% of AURA-Cls texts appear verbatim in AURA-Sentiment and are grouped with them.
  * A spot-check at cosine 0.85–0.88 found formulaic variants, so the grouping is conservative. No quantitative spot-check record exists. [R]

### 6.3 Pre-registered selection rule (`configs/a4_selection_rule.yaml`, commit `e541832`, before training)

1. Rank candidates by validation e2e macro-F1; transformers are ranked by their three-seed mean.
2. A transformer replaces TF-IDF only if its gain is ≥ 0.02 **and** its gate macro-F1 drops by no more than 0.01.
3. Synthetic data is retained only if it gains ≥ 0.005 for the same architecture.
4. Ties (< 0.005) go to the cheaper model.
5. Export the median seed.
6. `needs_review` thresholds are chosen on validation for ≈ 0.90 automatic-decision precision.
7. The holdout is evaluated once, after the selection is committed.

## 7. Evaluation protocol

* **Metrics.** Macro-F1 is the primary metric (e2e for A4). Per-class P/R/F1 with support, confusion matrices, accuracy and review statistics are also reported. Definitions are in `ALL_EXPERIMENTAL_RESULTS.md` (glossary).
* **Uncertainty.**
  * Percentile bootstrap: 2,000 resamples, seed 42.
  * Paired bootstrap Δ only for the pre-registered E4 vs E0 contrast.
  * No correction for multiple comparisons; the other contrasts are descriptive.
* **One-time evaluations.**
  * v1 test: once, in A2. Its rerun was only a reproduction check.
  * A4 holdout: once, in `1d3cbfa`, after the selection commit `2b15bc4`.
* **External proxy.** AURA-Classification human `bug_report` labels for the gate, on groups never used in training (n = 2,604).
* **Abstention.** `needs_review` is raised when the complaint score falls in the gate band or the category score is below the category threshold. The reported quantities are review rate, auto coverage and auto accuracy.
* **What was never evaluated.**
  * Dialect or per-app bias.
  * Per-domain metrics.
  * Leave-one-app-out.
  * Held-out preprocessing ablation.
  * Actionability vs sentiment (RQ5).
